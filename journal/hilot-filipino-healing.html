Hilot, in long form: the Filipino healing massage we still trust

May 2, 2026 · Manang Lourdes, senior practitioner · 11 min read

I learned Hilot from my grandmother in a village outside Banga in the early 1980s, and I have been practicing it for thirty-eight years. The clinic asked me to write down what I do, because most of what is published online about Hilot — even by Filipinos — gets parts of it slightly wrong. Some of those small wrongs gather into a larger one.

Hilot is the Tagalog word for "to set right." That is the closest definition I can offer in a single phrase. It is a healing massage, but the word "massage" carries the wrong connotations. Massage in the Western sense is technique applied to muscle. Hilot is a diagnosis followed by a response, where the response is shaped to what the diagnosis revealed. If a Western massage is a song, Hilot is a conversation.

The diagnosis

The first part of a Hilot session is the diagnosis. It is the part most newcomers do not know about. Before any oil is applied or any pressure is given, we lay strips of banana leaf along the spine and along the back of the legs. The leaves are warmed first, briefly, over heat or by hand.

What we are reading is the temperature of the leaf as it sits on the body. Where the leaf cools quickly, blood flow is poor — that is where tension lives. Where the leaf darkens or curls slightly, the inflammation underneath is reaching the surface. Where the leaf stays evenly warm, the body is in balance and we will leave that area alone.

People sometimes ask whether this is "real," meaning whether it would survive a journal study. I will say what I told my granddaughter when she went to nursing school: the leaf reads what the hand could read alone, but it makes the reading visible to both of us. The technique trains the practitioner more than it diagnoses the patient. It is not a thermometer; it is a tutorial.

"The leaf reads what the hand could read alone. But it makes the reading visible — to both of us."

The oil

We use virgin coconut oil. It is from Aklan, often pressed within forty kilometres of where we apply it. We do not use mineral oil, scented oil, or commercial blends. The reason is partly tradition; the reason is mostly that virgin coconut oil is the right molecular size to penetrate the skin's lipid barrier, carrying with it the natural anti-inflammatory compounds (lauric acid, polyphenols) that cold-pressed coconut produces.

The oil is warmed in the hand, never in a microwave. Body-temperature oil is more comfortable on tense skin and reads pressure better — overheated oil dulls the practitioner's sensitivity to the muscle underneath.

The strokes

I will describe four. There are more, but these four are the bulk of any Hilot session.

Hagod

Long, slow strokes along the meridian of the back, from the base of the neck to the lower back, following the spine but never touching it directly. Hagod opens circulation and prepares the deeper muscles for the work that comes after. It is the stroke most resembles "Swedish" massage, but it is gentler — the goal is invitation, not extraction.

Pisil

Pinch-and-release pressure on identified knots. The pinch is held for a slow count of three, released for a slow count of two, repeated. We use the side of the thumb, never the tip. Pisil is the working stroke of the session — most of the relief a guest reports the next day comes from this part.

Hilot proper

The diagnostic stroke. The hand reads the body as it travels — not pressing into the tissue, but reading the resistance the tissue offers back. Where the hand finds pockets of tightness it has not previously identified, the practitioner pauses, reapplies pisil, and continues. This is the stroke that takes thirty years to learn well, because it is mostly listening with the palm.

Daok

The sealing stroke at the end of the session. Long, very gentle, downward — it is what tells the body the conversation is over. After daok, we usually advise five minutes of stillness before the guest sits up, so that the parasympathetic nervous system can stay settled.

"It is what tells the body the conversation is over."

What Hilot is good for

Hilot is genuinely useful for:

  • Chronic muscle tension, particularly in the upper back, neck, and lower back.
  • Mild headaches of muscular origin.
  • Stress-related sleep difficulty.
  • The dull discomfort of long flights, long days at a desk, and long stretches of unprocessed grief — these last show up in the body whether or not the mind has caught up.
  • Mild postpartum recovery — though we use a modified protocol and would prefer to coordinate with your obstetrician.

What Hilot is not for

I will be plain. Hilot is not a substitute for medical care. It is a complement to it. We have referred guests to Dr. Magallanes and to the hospital when something we found under our hand suggested it should be looked at by a doctor.

We do not perform Hilot on:

  • Active fractures or unhealed surgical sites.
  • Active infection or fever.
  • Confirmed deep vein thrombosis or active cardiac instability.
  • Pregnancy, except with explicit obstetrician clearance and a modified protocol.
  • Cancer, except in palliative coordination with the treating physician.

If any of these applies and you would still like to come in, please call rather than book online. We will talk and we will figure something out, even if what we figure out is "later."

How Aurum keeps the practice clean

The clinic is the modern part. The Hilot is the old part. We try to honour both:

  • Banana leaves are sourced fresh, daily, from the same family who supply the clinic restaurant in town. They are inspected before use and disposed after a single session.
  • The virgin coconut oil is decanted into a sealed dispenser at the start of every shift. It is never returned to a shared bottle.
  • Linens are hospital-grade and laundered at temperatures that match clinic standards.
  • The treatment room is cleaned between every session — not "tidied," cleaned — and you are welcome to ask to see the schedule.

The hygiene is not in tension with the heritage. My grandmother would have approved of all of it. She was a careful woman.

What a session feels like

You will undress to your comfort and lie on a warm sheet. The leaf diagnosis takes a few minutes and is silent; the practitioner will speak only if a finding warrants it. The oil is warm, and the strokes begin slow. Some guests fall asleep in the first ten minutes; some breathe deeply and weep, quietly; some stay awake and let the hour pass. Any of these is fine.

At the end, we ask you to drink water. Hilot moves things — fluid, blood, sometimes feelings — and water is how the body completes the work after we leave the room.
