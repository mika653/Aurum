Your first Botox in Aklan: what a doctor-led clinic actually does differently

April 12, 2026 · Dr. J. Magallanes, M.D., DPBA · 14 min read

People come to us for a first Botox appointment from one of two places: a friend's recommendation, or an Instagram ad. The two routes produce very different conversations. The friends arrive with a face they want to look like; the ads arrive with a face they want not to. We can work with both, but the second often takes longer — because before any neurotoxin is dosed, we have to undo a sales pitch.

This article is not a sales pitch. It is a candid description, written by the doctor who runs the clinic, of what your first Botox appointment looks like at Aurum in Kalibo, Aklan, and what the difference between a doctor-led injection and a non-medical one really amounts to in practice. If you are weighing your options, please read it before booking — anywhere.

What "doctor-led" actually means

The phrase has been over-used by clinics that mean only that a doctor signed the licence. At Aurum, doctor-led means the same physician who consulted with you holds the needle, draws the dose, and reviews you fourteen days later. There is no other person in the chain of custody for the product.

This matters because Botox is a prescription neurotoxin. The dose is small but the muscle map is small too — a difference of a few units placed a few millimetres apart can be the difference between rested-looking and surprised-looking. Pharmacology and anatomy are how doctors are trained to think; sales targets are how clinics often instruct injectors. Pick the one you want around your eyes.

"You are not buying units. You are buying judgment, applied to a face."

What we do before any product is opened

Your first appointment runs sixty minutes, of which only ten are the actual injection. The remaining fifty look like this:

  1. Medical history. Pregnancy, breastfeeding, autoimmune disease, neuromuscular disorders (myasthenia gravis, Lambert-Eaton), prior aesthetic procedures, and any medication that thins blood — all explicit screens. We will reschedule rather than dose if any of these is unresolved.
  2. Photographs. Front, three-quarter, and profile, taken under matched lighting. We will compare these against day-14 images. Without baseline photographs, "results" are anecdote.
  3. Anatomical mapping. We mark, in pencil, every planned injection site on your face, and we explain what each one does. You can ask us to remove any of them. Most patients do remove one or two; we are usually grateful when they do.
  4. Dose plan. The total unit count for your face. Written down. Quoted in pesos against the unit price, not as a flat package. You take this paper home; we keep a copy in your file.
  5. Consent. The standard medical consent form, given in English and explained in Filipino if you prefer. Risks are read aloud; we do not skip the asterisks.

If at any point during these fifty minutes you feel rushed, the clinic has failed you. Walk away. Botox is not an emergency, and any clinic suggesting otherwise has confused urgency for revenue.

The injection itself

The needle is small (32G, the size used for diabetic insulin pens). The procedure is brief. We use a topical numbing cream and an ice pack. The sensation is closer to a cold pinch than a "shot." Bleeding is rare and usually a single drop; bruising is possible at sites with small surface vessels and resolves within five to seven days.

What is happening pharmacologically: Botox blocks the acetylcholine release at the neuromuscular junction of the muscles we have targeted. The muscle relaxes and stops creating the dynamic line on the surface of the skin. It does not erase the line itself. If a line has become static — etched into the skin from years of repeated motion — Botox softens it but will not remove it. For static lines, we typically combine Botox with a low-dose filler or a mild laser, and we say so plainly during your consult.

Day 1 to day 14: what your face will do

Day 1

You will leave with small pinpoint marks at the injection sites; these resolve within an hour or two. You may not look in a mirror until then if you prefer; we have hot tea and the journal you are now reading. We ask you to:

  • Avoid lying flat for four hours.
  • Avoid strenuous exercise, alcohol, and saunas for twenty-four hours.
  • Avoid pressure or massage on the treated areas for forty-eight hours.

These are not superstitions. They reduce the small risk of product migration before binding completes.

Day 3 to day 5

The product begins to take effect. You may feel a subtle heaviness in the brow or forehead — this is normal and resolves as binding completes. You will start noticing that you cannot fully furrow your brow when you frown, but the face at rest still looks like yours.

Day 7

You will see the meaningful change. People who know you well may say "you look rested" without identifying why. People who do not know you well will not notice anything has happened. This is the goal.

Day 14

The follow-up appointment. We compare day-14 photographs to baseline. If any pocket of muscle was under-dosed, we top up — at no charge, because the original quote covered it. The new photographs become the baseline for your next visit.

"You should look like yourself, on a day you slept well."

What we will refuse

This is the part most clinics edit out. There are appointments we decline, and we want you to know which ones.

  • Patients under 21 for cosmetic Botox. Faces still mature into the early twenties; we do not chase a problem that has not arrived. Therapeutic Botox (for migraine, hyperhidrosis) is assessed on its own merits.
  • Total volume requests above what your face needs. If you ask us for fifty units in your forehead, we will give you what we plan, not what you ask, and we will explain why. If that is unacceptable, we will refund your consult fee and recommend Iloilo.
  • "Frozen" looks. The aesthetic ideal that erases all expression is not part of our practice. We can introduce you to clinics that do offer it. We will not pretend to share the philosophy.
  • Same-day procedures. Even if the slot is open, we will book you in for a second visit. You should sleep on it. Anyone who thinks twenty-four hours of reflection is unreasonable for a prescription medication has business interests we don't share.

What you should ask, anywhere you go

Whether you book with us or not, ask these five questions before any clinic injects you:

  1. Who, by name, will perform the procedure? Is that person on premises today?
  2. What is the brand of product you will use, and what is the lot number? (We display the open vial in front of you.)
  3. What is the unit-level price, and how many units do you plan to use?
  4. What is your protocol if I have an adverse reaction in the next forty-eight hours?
  5. What does my fourteen-day follow-up look like?

If a clinic stumbles on any of these — particularly the first and the fifth — politely leave.

The cost

We do not publish a flat price for Botox because no two faces require the same dose. What we will publish is the per-unit cost, which is anchored to the wholesale price of the genuine product (we use Allergan's Botox; we display the box). Most first-time patients use between 20 and 40 units across forehead, glabella, and crow's feet. Expect to budget meaningfully — and to budget less than you would for a flight to Manila for the same procedure.

Aurum holds Botox prices steady for our Gold Circle Devotee members; outside the membership, the price is the price. We do not offer flash sales on prescription medication.

The clinic itself

We are at Poblacion, Kalibo, five minutes from the airport. A consultation is sixty minutes; we will hand you a written plan and you may take it elsewhere. There is no obligation to proceed. Many of our best patient relationships started with a quote we lost on price and won back on judgment a year later.

If you have a flight to Boracay and an open afternoon, you can build the consultation into your travel day. We will be honest about whether your timeline allows for the procedure itself before you board the ferry. (Usually, it does not — Botox before travel is a poor idea, and we will say so.)

One last thing

The face we want you to leave Aurum with is one that looks like yourself — on a day you slept well, drank water, and didn't carry the past month into your forehead. That is the only outcome we know how to produce, and we are happy to produce it for you.

If you were nervous about a first appointment, we hope you are now equipped with the right questions. Whether you ask them of us or of someone else is, frankly, fine.
